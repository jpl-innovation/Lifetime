# 1.3.0 server uptime: what the docs must say

Worker 4 (server uptime). For the lead to merge into README.md, RELEASE_NOTES.md ("Next release"), SECURITY.md and
docs/SERVER_OPERATIONS.md. Plain words, for staff and IT.

## Release notes (Next release)

- **Uptime from any date.** The server dashboard has one date range control: Last 7 days, Last 30 days, This month,
  Last month, This year, Last 12 months, Last 5 years, All, or Custom with From and To calendars (no lower limit; To
  cannot be in the future; at most 10 years at once). It is used for:
  - a computer's **Awake time per day** (replaces the 14 / 30 days box). Long periods show one bar per week (over 3
    months) or per month (over 2 years): the average awake time of the days that have a record;
  - the **Overview** fleet totals ("Daily awake hours", replaces Last 7 / Last 30 days), with the total for the
    period and how many computers contributed;
  - the **Energy** page (energy, cost, top consumers and groups for the period; "after hours" stays the last 7 days);
  - the **Export report** dialog (All = all available history, as before).
  Days with no record stay empty (grey / unknown), never zero.
- **Uptime & downtime of one computer** (Devices, computer detail, under Awake time per day, same period): per day
  (or week / month) awake, asleep, off and unknown time; restarts, clean shutdowns and unexpected stops (Kernel-Power
  41 / EventLog 6008); the awake share of the known time; awake time per unexpected stop; where the data starts
  ("Data from … (Windows installation date) · recorded by Lifetime since …"); and **availability in expected hours**
  from server monitoring with the number of outages and the longest one. Asleep / off / stops come only from
  computers with Lifetime 1.3 or later; an older computer shows its awake time and the rest of each day as Unknown.
- **Fleet uptime bars** (Overview, "Uptime by computer"): one row per computer, one small bar per day (per week over
  3 months, per month over 2 years), coloured by availability in expected hours: good (99 % or more), partial (75 to
  99 %), down (under 75 %), unknown (monitoring saw less than the coverage floor, 80 % by default), outline = not
  expected online (for example a laptop without a schedule). Worst first, 50 at a time ("Show more computers"). Hover
  a bar for its numbers. Order: computers with enough evidence by availability (lowest first), then those with too
  little evidence, then those not expected online.
- **Reports for any period, compared with the previous period.** Server reports (PDF/CSV) for a computer, a group or
  the fleet have an "Uptime & downtime" section: awake, asleep, off and unknown hours, restarts, clean shutdowns,
  unexpected stops, computers that report their off time, availability in expected hours, outages and the longest
  outage, each shown as "value (previous period: value)" for the period of the same length just before. Group and
  fleet reports add awake hours, unexpected stops and availability per computer. "All history" has no comparison and
  says so.
- **Keep detailed records for** (Administration › Data retention, default 400 days, 30 to 3,660): monitoring's raw
  online/offline history, incident timelines and sent-alert records older than this are removed automatically
  (before 1.3 this was a fixed 180 days and removed whole incidents). Incident summaries are kept. Raw performance
  readings were already kept for 7 days only and still are; the daily battery/disk readings used for replacement
  forecasts stay 2 years; neither follows this setting. Daily totals
  (awake time per day, the off-time breakdown per day) and the computers' awake-time records are never trimmed
  automatically. **"Delete records before" now also removes monitoring's history, incidents, maintenance windows and
  daily readings before that date** (before 1.3 it left monitoring alone).

## How it works (SERVER_OPERATIONS)

- Protocol: a 1.3 computer may send `history.downtime_daily` (per local date: `asleep_s`, `off_s`, `unknown_s`,
  `restarts`, `shutdowns`, `unexpected_stops`, all whole numbers ≥ 0; at most 3,660 days). The server advertises the
  capability `downtime`; computers send it only to servers that advertise it (`lifetime_protocol.without_extended`
  strips it otherwise) and only when history sharing is on. The server refuses the whole sync if the field is
  malformed: wrong type, missing or extra field, negative or fractional value, a count over 10,000, a bad date, more
  than 3,660 days, or a day whose asleep + off + unknown + that day's awake time (from `history.daily`) is over
  90,000 s (a 25-hour DST day plus slack). It is stored with the computer's history, removed with it when history
  sharing is turned off, trimmed by "Delete records before", and checked when a backup is restored.
- Availability is the existing model (lifetime_availability: expected hours from the schedule, maintenance excluded,
  unknown never counted as up or down). An **outage** is one stretch of observed offline time that overlaps the
  expected hours; its length is the expected-hours time it covers (offline overnight between two working days is
  one outage of 2 h if it covers the last hour of one day and the first of the next).
- Monitoring now answers periods of up to 3,660 days (was 400). Availability for days older than "Keep detailed
  records for" is Unknown, because the raw online/offline history is gone; there is no daily availability rollup
  yet (see Not done).
- New read-only admin operations: `uptime_bars` (start, end; optional kind, offset, limit ≤ 100, as_of) and
  `detail_retention`; new Admin operation `set_detail_retention` (days). `fleet` takes optional `start`/`end`,
  `insights` optional `start`/`end`. Every admin reply stays under the 1 MiB limit (checked for 10 years of daily and
  downtime data for one computer, and for 100 computers × 366 daily bars).
- Speed, measured on the development PC with synthetic data (500 computers, about 3 online/offline changes a day,
  550,000–600,000 presence intervals over 400 days): the server computes the fleet bars in 2.1 s for 365 daily bars,
  1.4 s for 53 weekly bars (Last 12 months), 2.2 s for Last 5 years and 2.0 s for 10 years (monthly bars); later
  pages come from a one-minute cache in about 1 ms. The dashboard classifies and paints all 500 rows × 365 bars in
  0.5–0.8 s (only the rows in view are painted when scrolling). The availability computation is now one sweep per
  computer (O(n+m)) that adds time straight into the day/week/month bars; the old per-day O(n×m) computation alone
  took 12–16 s for 500 computers × 365 days. With 500 computers each sending 5 years of daily and off-time totals,
  the Overview fleet totals take 0.2–0.35 s and the Energy page's inputs 0.3 s (only the awake parts of each history
  are parsed). A computer's detail with the largest history a computer may send (10 years of daily, imported and
  off-time totals, hourly detail, sessions, 100 records, 24 h of readings) is 668 KB, under the 1 MiB reply limit.
  Python only, no Rust. While the bars are computed (about 2 s) monitoring's lock is held, so a monitoring pass waits.

## SECURITY

- No new data leaves a computer unless it runs 1.3, history sharing is on and the server advertises `downtime`.
  The new field is validated as strictly as the rest of the protocol.
- `uptime_bars` and `detail_retention` are read operations (Viewer and up); `set_detail_retention` is Admin only and
  audited like every other change. Monitoring never opens or writes the awake-time ledger; automatic trimming never
  touches the awake-time records or daily totals in server.sqlite.

## Not tested on real machines / known limits

- Not verified on real machines: everything above was tested with temporary databases, offscreen Qt and synthetic
  data only. A real 1.3 computer sending `downtime_daily` (worker 3) has not been paired with this server yet.
- The HTTP admin tests (test_server_runtime, the AdminTests in test_server_insights and test_server_access, and
  test_server_fleet's real-runtime dashboard tests) fail with HTTP 403 on the development PC even without these
  changes; CI must confirm them. The new operations were checked in-process through the runtime's own dispatch.
- Availability older than "Keep detailed records for" shows as Unknown (no daily availability rollup is kept).
- Day lengths for the off-time breakdown are 24 h (today: the hours so far); a DST day is not 23/25 h there.
- The Energy page's "after hours" figures stay the last 7 days (they need hourly history, kept 120 days).
- The new dashboard asks a server before 1.3 for the fleet totals without a period (last 7 days); the fleet bars,
  long availability periods and the period's energy are then unavailable.

## After the review (update the text above where it differs)

- A computer's detail reply no longer carries `history.downtime_daily`, only `downtime` (first, last, days); the
  new read-only admin call `uptime_days` (device_id, start, end) serves the awake and off-time days of a period.
  Readings in the detail are thinned to at most 400. Tested with 10 years of history and a reading every 30 s.
- A sync without the extended history (a 1.3 computer falling back to the older format) keeps the stored
  off-time days and activity detail until a new valid set arrives. Refusals of the field start with
  "Invalid downtime history".
- Over the monitoring size quota: sent alerts, then resolved incidents' timelines, then the oldest resolved
  incident summaries go before recent presence. Summaries are kept 10 years at most.
- "Delete records before" refuses a date in the future (store and monitoring); the date field stops at today; the
  confirmation lists what goes and what stays (latest totals, open incidents, everything from the date on).
- A restored out-of-range "Keep detailed records for" is ignored (default 400 days).
- Periods start on 1 January 1971 or later (pickers and monitoring); a refused period shows "not available"
  instead of falling back to the last 7 / 30 days. A report whose previous period would start before 1971 has no
  comparison and says so.
- Long-period fleet bars on the Overview are the average per recorded day. A DST day counts 23 or 25 hours.
- Fleet uptime bars: computed outside the monitoring lock per computer; at most 36,000 bars per page (fewer
  computers per page for years of daily bars); a period chosen while bars load is asked next; "Show more" keeps
  the first page's "as of".
